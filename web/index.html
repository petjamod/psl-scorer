How to take a scoreable photo
  • Back camera, not the selfie camera.
  • Arm's length or further. Close ups distort the face (bigger nose, narrower face).
  • Face straight on, camera at eye level, head not turned or tilted.
  • Neutral face: mouth closed, no smile.
  • Even front light, for example facing a window. No harsh shadows.
  • No hat, no sunglasses, hair off the face.
What this measures

It predicts the average rating the SCUT-FBP5500 raters would give this photo (60 raters per face, scale 1 to 5). The model is a ResNet18 checked against held out human ratings: Pearson correlation around 0.9 on 2,200 test faces.

It is not an objective truth. It reflects the taste of that rater group, and the training faces are mostly young Asian and Caucasian adults. Different photos of the same face can score differently because of light, lens distance, angle and expression.

PSL and 1 to 10 are simple rescalings of how far the raw score sits from the average SCUT face of the selected gender (PSL = 4 + z, 1 to 10 = 5 + 1.5 z). The percentile compares against the SCUT training faces of that gender.
